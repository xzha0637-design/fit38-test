What the model looks at (Public-data feature schema)

What the 12 public-data features mean

These are signals used together by the model. No single feature proves that an account is automated, harmful or genuine.

Account age
Days since the public account creation date. A newer or older account is context, not proof.
Follower count
How many accounts follow this account. It gives scale but does not show follower quality.
Following count
How many accounts this account follows. The model compares it with other activity signals.
Follower-to-following ratio
Follower count divided by following count. It shows balance between audience and connections.
Tweet count
The recorded total number of public posts made by the account.
Posting frequency
Average public posts per day over the account's recorded age.
Description length
Number of characters in the public biography, not an analysis of what the biography says.
Username length
Number of characters in the public username. The wording of the username is not analysed.
Profile completeness
How many required model fields are available. Below 50% stops scoring as Insufficient data.
Verified status
Whether the public verified flag is present. Verification alone does not determine risk.
Default profile image
Whether the account still uses the platform's default profile image.
Description present
Whether a public biography is present, without judging its content.

Full follower/following graph features, graph topology, private data, and content-semantic features are excluded.

What the numbers mean

Available evaluation evidence

Held-out test split: 5,929 records. At the recorded XGBoost binary threshold of 0.10:

F1
0.5859
Precision
0.4158
Recall
0.9915
Calibration evidence
Low/Medium/High boundaries were selected on a separate validation split of 5,929 records (recorded validation mean cost: 0.266149). No formal probability-calibration or reliability metric is currently available.

Plain-language reading

  • F1: one summary of the balance between precision and recall; higher is better, but it does not show which errors occurred.
  • Precision: about 42 of every 100 accounts assigned the recorded positive class matched the positive label in this test data.
  • Recall: about 99 of every 100 positive-labelled accounts in this test data were found at the recorded threshold.

A risk score is a ranking signal, not a probability. For example, a score of 91% does not mean there is a 91% chance that the account is a bot.

These figures describe the bundled datasets and are not evidence of performance on current live or external populations.

Known risks and unsupported uses

  • Cross-dataset limits: performance has not been validated on an independent external dataset.
  • Prevalence shift: different bot/human proportions can change precision and error rates.
  • Concept drift: account behaviour and platform conventions can change after the evaluation data period.
  • False positives: legitimate accounts may receive elevated scores; the recorded precision shows this risk is material.
  • Excluded graph evidence: coordinated-network signals cannot be inferred from this feature set.

Do not use the output as proof that an account is automated, harmful, fraudulent, or policy-violating. Do not use it for automated moderation, sanctions, reporting, or enforcement.
